A short web address is not a manual

Every major printer now ships with one line of instruction printed on a folded sheet. Here is what actually sits behind each of those addresses, and what to have in front of you before you start typing.

Printer boxes used to come with a booklet. Now they come with a web address, a power cable, and the assumption that you will work out the rest. It's a reasonable assumption most of the time. The trouble starts when the installer can't find the printer, or finds it and then loses it, and the folded sheet has nothing else to say.

What follows is the short version of where each brand sends you and what that page will and won't do for you.

Canon

Canon prints ij.start.canon on the quick start sheet for almost everything it sells, from the cheapest home all-in-one up to the office machines. The page asks for a model number and then hands you a package for your operating system.

One detail matters more than it looks: use the model string from the label on the machine, not the name on the carton. Canon's retail names and its technical model names drift apart, and picking the wrong one gets you software that installs perfectly and then never finds the printer.

HP

HP's route is 123.hp.com/setup, and it behaves differently from the others. Rather than a standalone driver, HP mostly pushes you toward its app, which carries the driver inside it and handles the wireless pairing as part of the walkthrough.

Watch the enrollment checkbox during an HP install. Opting into HP+ commits the printer to original HP cartridges for the rest of its life and requires it to stay connected to the internet. It cannot be undone afterwards, and it goes past most people in about two seconds.

Epson

An Epson Printer Setup usually runs through Epson's own setup utility on a computer, or the Smart Panel app on a phone. Epson splits its software more than the other brands do, so it's worth knowing that the everyday scanning tool and the detailed scanning tool are two separate programs, and a quick install often brings only one of them.

Brother

A Brother Printer Setup gives you a choice the others tend to hide: a full software package, or a driver on its own. The driver-only option is genuinely useful if you want the printer without the monitoring tools, but it leaves scanning behind, which catches people out weeks later when they finally need to scan something.

Have these ready before you start

  • The model number from the label on the printer, not the box.
  • Your Wi-Fi password, typed rather than remembered.
  • Your router's 2.4 GHz network. Most home printers cannot join 5 GHz at all.
  • Mobile data switched off, if you are setting up from a phone. The printer's temporary network has no internet, and phones abandon it mid-setup.
  • A printed network configuration page once you're done. It lists the printer's address, and you'll want it the next time something goes quiet.

None of this is difficult. It's just none of it is on the sheet in the box, which is the whole reason independent guides exist.
