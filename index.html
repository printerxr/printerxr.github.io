The install choice nobody explains

Every printer brand offers more than one way to install, and the box never says which one you're picking. Choose wrong and printing works fine while scanning quietly never arrives.

Find your printer on PrinterXR Setup guides for Canon, HP, Epson and Brother across Windows, macOS, ChromeOS, Linux, iOS and Android.

Most people install a printer once and never think about it again, which is exactly why the choice made during those two minutes causes trouble months later. The installer offers options, everyone clicks the recommended one, and the difference only surfaces the day someone needs to scan a form.

There are three ways to get a printer working, and they are not equivalent.

  1. The full software package Everything the brand makes for that model. Printing, scanning, ink monitoring, firmware updates, and usually a utility for alignment and head cleaning. Biggest download, most background processes, and the only option that reliably gives you scanning.
  2. The driver on its own Just enough to print. Small, quiet, no extra software running at startup. Fine for a laser printer you only ever print from, useless if the machine has a scanner you intend to use.
  3. No driver at all Modern operating systems can print to most network printers with nothing installed. Apple calls it AirPrint, Android has an equivalent built in, and Windows will often add a printer it discovers on the network by itself. Convenient, but you lose tray selection, duplex control, and scanning.

The rule of thumb is simple. If the machine has a scanner lid, install the full package. If it doesn't, the driver alone will serve you better and leave your system cleaner.

How each brand presents the choice

ij.start.canon

Canon leads with a recommended bundle and puts the individual components lower down the same page. The scanning utility is one of those separate components, so a quick install can leave it out without ever saying so.

123.hp.com/setup

HP has mostly collapsed the choice into a single app that carries the driver inside it. Simpler, but it means the app is not optional in the way people assume, and removing it takes printing with it.

Epson Printer Setup

Epson splits its tools further than anyone else. There is a basic scanning app and a detailed one, and they install separately. If the scan window looks stripped back compared to what a guide describes, you probably have the other one.

Brother Printer Setup

Brother is the most honest about it, offering the full package and a driver-only download side by side. That transparency is useful, though it does mean people choose driver-only without realising scanning is part of what they declined.

Already installed and scanning doesn't work?

You don't need to remove anything first. Run the full installer over the top of what's already there, and the missing pieces get added without disturbing the printer you've already set up.

What the driverless route is actually good for

It's tempting to skip installation entirely, and for a second computer in the house that's the right call. A laptop that prints a boarding pass twice a year does not need software from a printer manufacturer running on it permanently.

Treat it as a supplement rather than a replacement. One machine in the house gets the full package and handles scanning and maintenance. Everything else prints driverless and stays clean. That split gives you the useful half of both options and is how most households should be set up anyway.

One thing to do after any install

Open your router's settings and reserve the printer's IP address so it always receives the same one. Printers get found during setup and then remembered by address, so when a power cut shuffles the addresses around, every computer in the house loses the printer at once. Two minutes now, and the install you just finished keeps working.
